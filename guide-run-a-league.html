How to run a fair online gaming league (host guide)

SINLESS GG Guides · Updated 28 October 2026

A good league is not just fixtures and a table. It is clear rules, fair decisions and players who feel respected. This guide shares what works for hosts on SINLESS GG, from the first announcement to crowning the champion.

1. Write short, clear rules

Players read short rules. Cover the essentials in a few lines:

  • Match length and game settings (for example, team strength limits).
  • How and when to report scores, and what proof you need.
  • What happens after the deadline: forfeit, double forfeit or extra time.
  • How disconnects are handled.
  • Behaviour: no insults, no cheating, no fake screenshots.

On SINLESS GG players accept your rules when they join, so nobody can say they did not know.

2. Choose the right size

Eight to twelve players is a comfortable size for a first league. Every extra player adds matches for everyone. If more people want to play, open a second division instead of making one huge table, and use promotion and relegation between them.

3. Set dates and play times

Pick a start and end date and the app spreads the matchdays evenly. Then set play times: the hours when matches should happen and how long each match takes, usually 10 to 15 minutes. Every fixture gets its own slot, nobody is booked twice at the same time, and players get reminders in the morning and 30 minutes before their slot.

Tip: evening hours work best for most players. Avoid school and work hours.

4. Colour your table

Use table zones to mark the top places (for example, Champions League spots) and the relegation places. Players fight harder when they can see what is at stake, and the table looks great when shared.

5. Keep things moving

The Unplayed tab on the fixtures board shows every match that should have been played. For each one you can:

  • Nudge both players with a reminder.
  • Record a forfeit for the player who did not show up (1–0).
  • Record a double forfeit in leagues, where nobody gets points.
  • Give extra time when both players ask for it.

Every decision asks you for a short reason and is saved in the match history. That record protects you if someone complains later.

6. Handle disputes calmly

When two players report different scores, look at the screenshots, ask both for their side, and decide. Explain your decision in one sentence. Consistency matters more than speed: treat the same situation the same way every time.

7. Use co-hosts

For bigger leagues, add one or two trusted co-hosts. They can approve scores, settle overdue matches and move fixtures, but they cannot change dates, rules or delete the tournament.

8. Finish the season properly

When every match is played, crown the champion. The winner receives a permanent title and the current-champion label. Share the final table picture, thank your players and announce the next season while everyone is still excited.

Cheating: if a title was won unfairly, the host can remove it with a reason, and the player is told why. Make it clear in your rules that this can happen.
